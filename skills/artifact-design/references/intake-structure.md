# Purpose, evidence, and information structure

Use this reference to reconstruct what an artifact must do, what its source supports, and how its content should be grouped or ordered.
It covers HTML products and linear documents; use the branch that fits the medium.

## Establish the working brief

Inspect the request, artifact, supplied data and sources, relevant project guidance, established content or design systems, and rendered output when available.
Record the medium, reader, likely task, desired outcome, important constraints, requested operation, and evidence that supports consequential claims.
For a mixed artifact, make a short task statement for each route or section with a materially different purpose.

Resolve conflicting criteria in this order: explicit user intent and authority; factual integrity and applicable access requirements; reader-task success; current documented brand constraints; then style preference.
Within that order, prefer supplied source material and current project documentation, direct inspection, repeated conventions, and cautious inference.
State unresolved conflicts instead of blending incompatible requirements.
Separate confirmed decisions from observed patterns.
An interface can contain an intentional older treatment, an accidental inconsistency, or an undocumented exception; one screenshot rarely settles which.
Mark assumptions that could change the work and keep reversible choices provisional.
Ask a focused question only if missing information changes authority, factual meaning, protected content, or the requested outcome.

Never invent audience research, interviews, user preferences, analytics, business impact, approval, source ownership, or capabilities.
Functional outcomes may be inferred from the task; emotional or social motivations remain hypotheses without evidence.
Do not turn an inferred persona into a user finding.

## Set the operation boundary

- **Audit or review:** inspect and report; do not edit, reorganize, or silently rewrite.
- **Targeted repair:** correct the named issue and adjacent regressions within the granted scope.
- **Deslop or rewrite:** remove empty, generic, unsupported, or poorly organized material while keeping the writer's point, voice, terminology, necessary expertise, and qualifications.
- **Redesign or build:** change structure and visual treatment as needed for the requested outcome while preserving protected facts and behavior.
- **Mixed request:** set authority separately for each route or part.

An asset that looks inconsistent is not by itself permission to replace the whole identity or change product behavior.
Do not make a narrow correction into a full rebuild.

## Inventory and reconstruct structure

Before changing grouping or order, inventory the content that carries meaning.
Record its location, reader task, role, relationships, protected facts or behavior, and source status.
For a product, include routes, navigation, actions, content types, states, and alternate paths.
For a document, include headings, claims, evidence, figures, tables, captions, notes, citations, appendices, and the argument each section advances.

Map how a reader gets from a plausible entry point to the content or action they need.
Check whether labels predict their destination, categories reflect reader tasks rather than internal teams, prerequisite definitions appear in time, evidence supports the claim nearby, and return paths exist where useful.
Distinguish content model (what information and relationships exist), information architecture (how it is grouped and ordered), navigation (how an interface exposes paths), and source-file organization (how implementation is stored).
Do not use search as a substitute for a predictable structure.

For documents, write a one-line outline of each section's role: question, claim, evidence, explanation, consequence, limitation, method, or reference.
Put definitions before dependent reasoning and qualifications beside the affected claim.
Lead with a conclusion when the task and evidence support it; preserve a slower narrative when order, context, or voice carries meaning.

## Change only what the evidence supports

Describe a structure issue as `location / observed mismatch / likely cause / reader consequence / targeted repair`.
Mark cause and user effect as hypotheses unless source inspection or an exercised path confirms them.
Choose the smallest useful disposition: keep, move, rename, regroup, split, cross-link, consolidate, or remove.
Use tabs for parallel views, not as an arbitrary container for unrelated topics or a sequence of steps.
Add a table of contents, breadcrumb, local index, disclosure, or cross-link only when it solves a real orientation or retrieval problem and has a supported destination.
Counts of clicks, levels, choices, or headings are diagnostic clues, not universal pass/fail limits.

Keep useful density, technical terms, exceptions, legal language, source trails, and controls when they serve the reader.
Treat a content gap as a gap.
Do not fill an orphan with invented information or merge values that disagree without resolving their sources.

### Example: a sourced report

Suppose an existing report recommends a combined status view, reports 12 mismatches in a sample of 20, flags 3 possible duplicates that were not checked, and says the sample did not establish that mismatches caused delay.
Its current order opens with a recommendation and leaves the duplicate caveat in a distant appendix.

A task-led order could state what the sample shows, keep the duplicate and causality limits beside it, explain why a combined view is only a proposed test, then present the method and source trail.
Before and after, compare the denominator, count, caveat, recommendation status, and links.
Those example figures are synthetic; they are not evidence for a real report.

## Verify structure and preserve the record

Compare the revised artifact against the inventory.
Confirm every required fact, figure, unit, date, quotation, citation, link, qualification, meaningful section, action, destination, and state remains present and means the same thing.
Test changed anchors and navigation paths.
Read the new heading outline in order and walk important tasks from their entry points.
For screen work, inspect the actual reading order after reflow when possible.
For documents, continue with [documents](documents.md) for page sequence, export, and print behavior.

Report what was observed, inferred, changed, retained, and left unknown.
An outline or tidy sitemap is not evidence that readers were tested.
Use [QA parity](qa-parity.md) for the complete before/after comparison.
