# Editorial and explanatory pages

Use when the main job is understanding through sustained reading.
Define the reader's question and the sequence of ideas needed to answer it.
Place definitions before dependent arguments, distinguish evidence from interpretation, and keep qualifications beside the affected claim.
Headings should communicate the argument when scanned.
Use progressive disclosure for supplementary detail without concealing essential caveats.

Protect the reading column with comfortable measure, leading, contrast, and paragraph rhythm at actual font sizes.
Let the opening, illustration, captions, and surrounding frame express the subject's character without competing with the text.
Editorial work can be quiet, technical, playful, or photographic; a newspaper costume is not a requirement.

Give each figure an explanatory job, nearby introduction, useful caption, and appropriate alternative text.
Keep source links and footnote return paths understandable.
Use anchors and navigation for long pieces when they help readers resume or retrieve a section.
Do not interrupt a careful explanation with unrelated conversion panels.

Check an entire reading sequence at desktop, mobile, and zoom, including long headings, footnotes, figure reflow, and reduced motion if animation is present.
Verify that rearranging columns preserves the intended reading order.
Do not shorten away the necessary technical term merely to satisfy a readability heuristic.
