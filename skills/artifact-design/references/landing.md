# Landing pages

Use when a visitor must understand an offer and decide whether to act.
Identify the intended audience, actual offer, terms, supporting proof, likely objections, and destination of the primary action.
Build the sequence around that decision rather than filling a standard hero/features/testimonials template.
Make the first meaningful viewport explain what is offered and why this audience might need it.

Choose a concept connected to the real service, product, people, or material.
Authentic photography, a useful demonstration, strong typography, or a restrained composition can each be appropriate.
Use imagery only with verified rights; stock or generated imagery must not masquerade as the actual venue, product, customer, or completed work.
No testimonials, ratings, scarcity, customer logos, guarantees, or outcomes without evidence.
If proof is missing, identify what is needed and use the closest supported claim.

Put price, eligibility, dates, location, material exclusions, and other consequential terms where they inform the action.
Do not bury a limitation in a footer while promising the opposite above.
Match action labels to actual effects and destinations.
For forms, cover validation, submission, failure, success, and what happens next; label disconnected prototypes clearly.
Read [copy](copy.md) when drafting persuasive claims or form language.

Test the primary action and its destination, mobile first-screen clarity, long terms, image loading/crops, reduced motion, and keyboard use.
An attractive page with a dead booking action is unfinished.
