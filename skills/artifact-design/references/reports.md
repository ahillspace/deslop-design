# Reports

Use when a reader must assess evidence or make a decision.
Lead with the supported answer and its qualification; let comparisons, uncertainty, and methods follow in the order needed to assess it.
A decorative cover should not delay the finding.
Keep dates, scope, population, units, and source definitions near the claims they qualify.
Separate observation from explanation and recommendation.

Choose figures to answer specific questions.
Use comparable scales, honest baselines, labeled denominators, and consistent encodings; do not draw decorative trends from invented data.
Explain the important comparison in a caption and provide accessible values or an equivalent table.
Preserve missingness and uncertainty rather than implying precision.
Make methods and source detail reachable without crowding the main argument.

Use typography, figure grammar, annotations, and section rhythm to express identity.
Align numeric columns and support scanning across rows.
For wide tables, retain meaningful headers and offer a labeled keyboard-usable scroll region or an equivalent narrow view.
Do not hide evidence on mobile to simplify the composition.
Add long-page navigation when it helps retrieval; implement print behavior only when requested.

Verify a reader can find the conclusion, trace a figure to its data, understand an exception, and reach methods at narrow widths.
A report need not end with a sales action.
